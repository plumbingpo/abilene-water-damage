How Houston Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Houston, and they are worth learning. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Houston living room.

Typical Water Damage Problems in Houston

Most water damage calls we take in Houston trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. We see many of these in Houston homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. A lot of the mold we find in Houston comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.
